# Impeccable Design Context

## Design Context

### Users
Government officials and municipal planners — mayors, policy directors, urban sustainability teams — in Korean local government (Ulsan, Seosan, Suncheon). They use the tool both during desk-level analysis sessions and in formal stakeholder presentations (committees, briefings). Most have policy or administrative backgrounds, not technical/data science ones. The interface must make complex causal network models legible to someone who has never heard of a PEN matrix.

### Brand Personality
**Rigorous but fancy.** Three words: **authoritative, refined, transparent.**

This is not a corporate BI dashboard. It is closer in spirit to a beautifully typeset IPCC Working Group report or a premium consulting firm's policy brief — serious scientific authority delivered with considered visual elegance. The "fancy" comes from typographic precision, intentional spatial rhythm, and a restrained but confident use of color — not from decoration, animation, or flourish.

Emotional goal: when a user sees a simulation result, they should feel *"I trust this, and I can explain it to others."*

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Theme**: Light mode. Used in brightly lit offices and on projector screens in conference rooms. Light surfaces with high contrast.
- **Color**: Green as the primary brand hue — not lime/eco green, but a deep institutional green (think forest, not recycling bin). OKLCH-based. Neutrals tinted slightly toward the green hue for subconscious cohesion.
- **Anti-references**: Not an AI-generated page (no cyan/purple gradients, no glassmorphism, no blob shapes, no gradient text). Not a generic Korean government portal (no blue-heavy bureaucratic templates). Not a toy data app (no playful rounded cards, no emoji-heavy labels).
- **Typography**: A display typeface with scholarly weight for headings — something that would look at home on a UN report or a policy white paper. A clean, highly legible sans-serif for data labels and body. Both must render well in Korean and English.
- **Data visualization**: Plotly charts styled to match the brand palette — muted greens, warm neutrals, clean grid lines. Heatmaps should use a perceptually uniform diverging palette (not the default red-blue). Sankey/flow diagrams should feel like a publication figure, not a web toy.

### Design Principles
1. **Data is the hero** — the interface frames and amplifies the data; it never competes with it. Decoration exists only to clarify.
2. **Institutional authority** — every typographic and spatial decision should make the tool look like it belongs in a ministerial briefing room, not a startup demo.
3. **Progressive clarity** — lead with the conclusion (what changed, by how much), then let users drill into the causal path. Don't front-load complexity.
4. **Bilingual parity** — Korean and English must feel equally designed. No font-switching hacks, no size inconsistencies between scripts.
5. **Premium restraint** — "fancy" is expressed through spacing, weight contrast, and precise color — never through glow effects, gradients on text, or decorative chrome.
